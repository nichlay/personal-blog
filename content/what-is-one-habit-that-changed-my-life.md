So, believe it or not, walking in the morning without looking at my phone is one of the habits that changed my life.

Don’t believe it?

Here’s the story.

It all started in my first year as an undergraduate student. 

The first thing I did every morning is to look at my phone. I would either doomscroll on Instagram or watch YouTube videos. I don't know why, but looking at my phone feels "satisfying" at first. However, after a few days, my eyes started to feel strain, and going back to sleep became the only way to ease them. It wasn’t just my eyes, either. My brain felt “rotted” and "dysfunctional". In the end, looking at my phone only gave me a temporary satisfaction, not long-term refreshment.

Unfortunately, I was hardly aware of it until I watched a YouTube video (I can’t remember the title and the YouTuber’s name). Basically, the YouTuber and his friends challenged themselves to wake up early in the morning using an alarm clock instead of their phones, and they weren’t allowed to use their phones before 10 AM. They kept it up for a few days.

I was surprised to see that they were more focused yet refreshed. As a freshman, I wanted to change something about myself, so I decided to try it too.

The first few days weren’t easy. I had to hide my phone before going to sleep so I wouldn’t reach for it first thing in the morning. Then I didn’t know what to do after waking up. One day, I simply sat on my chair and stared at the plain white wall for a few minutes. Without my phone to distract me, it felt like I was hallucinating. It was so boring.

Eventually, I discovered that walking was my favorite thing to do. In my personal experience, walking boosts my energy, helps me think clearly, and lets my mind wander around freely. In one of Ali Abdaal's YouTube videos, <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EuVKpMRl7ik&t=450s">he explains that sunlight exposure can increase energy, help us feel more awake, and make us happier</a>. So, whenever possible, I went outside after waking up to get some sunlight. But honestly, just walking outside was enough to make me feel refreshed and focused.

It took me about 2 weeks to adapt to the “walking without a phone” morning routine. I’d say it’s worth your time to try. You can also try other activities that don’t involve phones or digital gadgets, such as reading physical books, writing in a notebook, exercising, cooking, or gardening. It doesn’t have to be walking like mine, as long as no digital gadgets are involved.

Now, on days with lectures or study sessions, I feel much more refreshed and focused than I used to.

Everyone is different, and the adjustment period will vary. However, at the end of the day, if you want long-term satisfaction, you have to pay a price for it. Scrolling gave me a quick reward every morning, but walking gave me something that lasted through the whole day. The key is to be consistent, keep your purpose in mind, and commit.

NOTE: I don’t follow their 10 AM rule strictly. It’s really hard not to look at my phone before then. My routine is simpler: after waking up, I take a short walk outside, then a shower, and only after that do I check my phone for emails and messages. They just inspired me to stop starting my mornings with mindless scrolling :)
